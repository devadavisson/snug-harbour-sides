From Julian's Kitchen
Radish Cake
Cantonese daikon and rice flour with pork sausage and dried shrimp. Steamed and seared, served cool.
Julian of the Drift -- the counter on the Long Run. The cake is finger food from the first minute, the thing that runs out first. Leave the Guinness alone beside it and let it do the work.
Soy-Braised Beef Shin
Star anise, three hours on the hearth. Off the bone and into a bun, cabbage slaw underneath.
The hearth dish. The pot sits on the Snug's fire three hours "and better for every one of them." He knew this one before he knew it. Built for hands, standing room -- everything held with a glass in the other hand.
Cabbage Slaw
Sharp, to cut the cake. Goes underneath the beef in the bun -- "which is better anyway."
It started beside the beef and moved under it on the second day of writing. The slaw found its place the way good ideas do: by being tried wrong first.
Frozen
Flaming Ice
Julian's jalapeno and lime ice cream. The challenge course.
His name for it. The heat and the cold at once -- the kitchen's answer to the question nobody asked, and the one everyone orders twice.
Blue Moon Glace
Butterfly pea and cream. Watch it turn violet when the acid hits.
Berthillon's original commission for Julian -- the signature-moment cone. The color shifts in front of you: blue to violet, one drop of citrus, the whole glass changes. Structural color you can taste.
The Unnamed Sorbet
Wild blackberry. Dark, structural, not simple-sweet. Taste it, name it, drop your name in the jar.
Sorbet de mure sauvage -- but tonight it has no name. Berthillon's third discipline: respect for the skin. Tannin-forward, wine-like, a cooling counter after the Flaming Ice that is still serious. The winning name, drawn at last call, becomes tomorrow's cone du jour.
Non-Alcoholic
Sour Plum
Julian's soft jug. Tart, cold, cuts through everything.
Crosses by water from the jetty in the same crate as the food. The soft option that isn't soft -- it holds its own against the beef.
Mint and Soda
Julian's second jug. Clean, bright, the palate reset.
The mint after the fire. The soda after the stout. The breath between courses that lets you taste the next one fresh.
Sparkling Pear Cider
Dry, effervescent. The non-alcoholic anchor.
The keeper's choice for the table. Light enough to carry all night, sharp enough to stand beside the food.
From the Taps
Oatmeal Stout
Dark, smooth, the pub's own gravity.
The weight in the glass. Against the radish cake it's "almost unfair" -- Julian's word. Leave it alone and let it do the work.
Bitter Ale
Served cool, not cold. The session pint.
The one you hold all night. Cool enough to taste, warm enough to linger. The publican's recommendation for the quiz rounds.
Dry Hard Cider
Handles the acid. The pairing the kitchen asked for.
Julian's table runs rich the whole way through -- pork, shellfish, beef, chilli. The cider cuts through all of it. Settled on the second day of letters between the kitchen and the bar.
Irish Whiskey
Neat. For after the toast.
The keeper drinks hard cider. But when the glasses go up and the room goes quiet the good way, the whiskey is what warms the silence after.
A Spot of Bitters
For anyone who gets the hiccups.
The oldest remedy on the shelf. Ask the tender. He's seen everything and says nothing.
