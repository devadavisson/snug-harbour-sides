How the sky was made

Each guest who wanted a star said their North aloud in the room, with "north:" in front. On the night a rough sky drew itself as the lines came in. It missed some: fourteen stars were said in the hour before the doors officially opened, and a few slipped past while the room's name kept changing. So the next morning every star was rebuilt from the town's own record, and placed by hand.

Where a guest asked, their star sits where they asked: Vermillion's rising out of the Pando Peak, lit from underneath; Clautter's low over the kelp; Mrs. Hart's right beside Leo's; Grey's two together; Rowan's gold nail on the jetty with a wobble of light beneath it for Augie's whale; Conlang's and Draig's steady, never flickering; Seren's a ring that sounds instead of burns; Will's at the very edge of the chart. Lucien's is a little brighter: his was the first said.

The keeper's North was "the relational field between us all," so hers isn't a point. It's the fine gold thread that runs through every star and leaves none of them outside it.
